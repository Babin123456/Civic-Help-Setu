# Encumbrance Certificate (EC) Application & Period Selection Guide

## Statutory Context
- Governed by the **Registration Act, 1908** (Section 57 - Inspection of Registration Books and grant of certified copies of entries) and State Registration Rules (e.g. **Tamil Nadu Registration Rules**, **Karnataka Registration Rules**, **Telangana / AP Registration Rules**).
- Issued by the **Sub-Registrar Office (SRO)** under the Department of Registration and Stamps (IGRS).

## Key Statutory Principles & Forms
1. **Nature of Document**:
   - An Encumbrance Certificate (EC) is a search extract from **Book 1 (Register of non-testamentary documents relating to immovable property)**.
   - It lists all registered transactions (sales, mortgages, leases, partition deeds, court attachments) executed and registered during a specified search window.
2. **Form 15 vs. Form 16 (Nil Encumbrance Certificate)**:
   - **Form 15 (Encumbrance Certificate)**: Issued when one or more registered transactions are found on the subject property during the search period. Details date of execution, deed nature, registered volume/page, executant, and claimant.
   - **Form 16 (Nil Encumbrance Certificate)**: Issued when no registered transactions affecting the property are found during the requested search window.
3. **What an EC Does NOT Cover (Critical Caveats)**:
   - **Oral Agreements or Unregistered Equitable Mortgages**: Deposit of title deeds with banks not formally registered in Book 1 (prior to state mandatory registration amendments).
   - **Revenue Litigation & Stay Orders**: Pending cases before Tehsildars, SDMs, or Civil Courts not registered or notified to the SRO.
   - **Testamentary Documents (Wills)**: Wills are registered in **Book 3** and kept strictly confidential during the testator's lifetime; they never appear on an EC.
   - **Unpaid Property Taxes or Utility Dues**: Does not reflect municipal lien or statutory tax arrears.
4. **Search Period Selection Best Practices**:
   - Standard minimum for bank home loans: **13 to 15 years**.
   - Standard for outright purchase or title search: **30 to 35 years** (tracing title back to parental/ancestral root).
   - Digitized vs. Manual Periods: Modern state portals (Kaveri 2.0, Inspector General of Registration Tamil Nadu / TNREGINET, IGRS Telangana) host digitized records typically from 1983-2004 onwards. Searching prior to digitization requires offline/manual SRO ledger search.
