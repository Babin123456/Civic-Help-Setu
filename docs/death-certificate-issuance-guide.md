# Death Certificate Issuance and Certified-Copy Guide — Research & Implementation Notes

**Prepared:** 5 November 2026  
**Related issue:** [#502 — Add a Death Certificate issuance and certified-copy guide](https://github.com/GoldRay19/Civic-Help-Setu/issues/502)  
**Scope:** India-wide statutory civil registration framework under the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969 (with central amendments effective October 2026) and state/UT civil registration procedures.  
**Status:** Citizen-facing research notes and structured data contract implementation.

---

## 1. What This Document Is and Is Not

- **Purpose & Legal Proof:** A Death Certificate is an official extract from the civil register of deaths maintained by the local Registrar of Births and Deaths. It is prima facie legal proof of the occurrence, place, and date of death under Section 12/17 of the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969.
- **What It Does NOT Prove:**
  - It does not establish legal heirship, survivorship, or property title.
  - It does not disclose the confidential medical cause of death for public use.
  - A civil death certificate is legally and structurally distinct from a Medical Certificate of Cause of Death (MCCD / Form 4/4A issued by an attending medical practitioner).

---

## 2. Who Can Apply & Informant Responsibilities

Registration responsibility is strictly determined by the **place of occurrence** (not the ancestral home, property location, or family's current residence):
- **Institutional Death (Hospitals, Nursing Homes, Health Centers):** The medical officer in charge or institutional head is statutorily responsible for sending the death reporting form (Form 2) to the jurisdictional Registrar.
- **Domiciliary / Home Death:** The head of the household, or the nearest relative present at the death, or the person causing the body to be disposed of, must report the event to the local Registrar within the statutory 21-day reporting window.
- **Public / Unclaimed / In-Transit Deaths:** The officer in charge of the local police station, village headman/chowkidar, or person finding the body must notify the local registration unit.

---

## 3. Statutory Timelines & Delayed Registration (2026 Legal Framework)

Under the Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969 as amended (effective 1 October 2026):
1. **Normal Reporting (Within 21 days):**
   - Must be reported within 21 days of occurrence.
   - Statutory registration is free of charge; one free copy of the certificate is delivered to the informant upon initial registration.
2. **Late Reporting (After 21 days up to 30 days):**
   - Allowed with late fee payment (prescribed by State rules, e.g. ₹100 in TN/state schedules).
3. **Delayed Reporting (After 30 days up to 1 year):**
   - Requires written permission of the prescribed authority (such as Block Development Officer / Executive Officer), payment of late fee, and an affidavit/declaration.
4. **Delayed Reporting (More than 1 year up to 2 years):**
   - Under Section 13(3) of the amended Act, requires an order of the District Magistrate (DM), Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM), or Executive Magistrate authorised by the DM.
5. **Delayed Reporting (More than 2 years):**
   - Under Section 13(3A) of the amended Act, requires an order of a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class (JMFC).

## 4. Required vs. Optional Documents

### A. Essential Documents
- Institutional Death Report / Medical Death Intimation (Form 2 / Form 4/4A) from hospital/institution, OR signed declaration by informant for domiciliary deaths.
- Disposal / Cremation / Burial ground receipt or undertaker receipt showing date and place of final rites.
- Identity proof of informant/applicant (Aadhaar, Voter ID, Passport) for identity verification.
- Proof of relationship of applicant to deceased (where applying for certified copies).

### B. Conditional / Delayed Registration Documents
- Non-Availability Certificate (NAC) issued by the jurisdictional Registrar (verifying that no prior death record exists).
- Judicial Magistrate / SDM order for events delayed beyond 1 year.
- Self-attested affidavit stating reasons for delay.

---

## 5. Distinction: Medical Cause of Death vs. Civil Death Certificate

- **Medical Certificate of Cause of Death (MCCD):** Kept strictly confidential for health and statistical analytics; not printed on the civil death certificate issued for public/financial settlements.
- **Certified Copy:** Under Section 17 of the Act, any family member or interested party can apply for extracts/certified copies of an already registered entry upon payment of prescribed search and copy fees.
