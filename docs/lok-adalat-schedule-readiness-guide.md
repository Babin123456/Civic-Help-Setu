# Lok Adalat Schedule & Settlement-Readiness Guide

## Statutory Foundations
**Lok Adalats (People’s Courts)** are statutory alternative dispute resolution (ADR) bodies established under **Chapter VI of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (Act No. 39 of 1987)**. They offer an expeditious, zero-cost, and amicable mechanism for resolving pre-litigation disputes and pending court cases.

---

## Key Statutory Pillars

### 1. Pre-Litigation vs. Pending Litigation
- **Pre-Litigation Stage**: Matters brought directly before Lok Adalat prior to filing formal suits in courts. Filing is free of court fees, preventing years of adversarial litigation.
- **Pending Litigation Stage**: Existing suits referred to Lok Adalat with the consent of parties or by court reference under Section 20(1).

### 2. The Mutual Voluntary Consent Rule
- The Lok Adalat functions as a conciliation and compromise body, **not an adjudicatory court**.
- Members of the bench cannot impose any decision or coercive order. A settlement occurs **only when all parties give voluntary, informed consent**.
- If either party refuses the terms, the matter is referred back to the regular court without prejudice.

### 3. Finality of Award (Section 21)
- Under **Section 21(1)** of the 1987 Act, every award made by a Lok Adalat is deemed to be a **decree of a civil court**.
- Under **Section 21(2)**, **NO APPEAL lies** to any court against the award of a Lok Adalat. The dispute attains absolute finality.

### 4. 100% Court Fee Refund
- Under **Section 16 of the Court Fees Act, 1870**, when a pending court proceeding is resolved through Lok Adalat, the full court fee deposited by the plaintiff is refunded.

## Eligible & Ineligible Subject Matters

### Highly Suitable Matters
- Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT) compensation disputes.
- Cheque dishonour complaints under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act.
- Bank recovery, credit card, and loan defaults (One-Time Settlement / OTS).
- Matrimonial disputes, maintenance, and child custody conciliation (excluding divorce decrees).
- Compoundable criminal offences under Section 320 CrPC / BNSS.
- Electricity, water, municipal tax, and mobile billing disputes.

### Statutorily Barred Matters
- **Section 19(5) Proviso**: Lok Adalats have **no jurisdiction** in respect of any matter or offence relating to non-compoundable criminal charges under any law.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can a party back out during Lok Adalat proceedings?
Yes. Until the settlement terms are mutually agreed, signed by all parties, and the award is pronounced by the bench, either party can withdraw consent without penalty or prejudice.

### 2. What if one party does not comply with the Lok Adalat Award?
Because Section 21 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 deems the Award a decree of a Civil Court, it is directly enforceable and executable through regular Civil Court execution proceedings (Order XXI of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908).

### 3. Is an advocate mandatory in Lok Adalat?
No. Parties can present their settlement terms directly in person before the conciliator bench without mandatory legal representation.

### 4. Are court fees refunded if the case is settled?
Yes. Under Section 16 of the Court Fees Act, 1870, a court fee refund certificate is issued to the party upon successful settlement of a pending court suit in Lok Adalat.

## National Lok Adalat Calendar
National Lok Adalats are organized quarterly across all judicial complexes in India (Taluk courts to Supreme Court) as per the annual schedule published by NALSA.
