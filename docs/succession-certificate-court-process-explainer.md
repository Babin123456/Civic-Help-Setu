# Succession Certificate Court Process Explainer (India)

## Statutory Context
- Governed by **Part X (Sections 370 - 390) of the Indian Succession Act, 1925**.
- Adjudicated by the **District Judge** or **Civil Judge (Senior Division)** having territorial jurisdiction over where the deceased resided at death or where property assets are located.

## Key Statutory Principles & Limitations
1. **Debts & Securities Only (Movable Assets)**:
   - Under Section 370 of the Indian Succession Act, a Succession Certificate is granted **strictly in respect of debts and securities** (bank accounts, fixed deposits, demat shares, debentures, government provident fund balances, mutual funds).
   - **Critical Caveat**: A Succession Certificate does NOT adjudicate or grant title in respect of **immovable property** (land, buildings, agricultural parcels). Immovable property inheritance requires probate, letters of administration, partition suit, or revenue mutation.
2. **Indemnity to Debtors (Section 381)**:
   - A certificate grantee affords full legal indemnity to banks, corporations, and debtors paying debts or transferring securities to the holder.
3. **Judicial Procedural Stages**:
   - **Petition Filing (Sec 372)**: Verified petition containing deceased details, time/place of death, family tree, absence of Will / intestacy statement, and full schedule of debts & securities.
   - **Public Citation (Newspaper Notice)**: Mandatory newspaper publication (one local language, one English daily) with a **30 to 45 day** returnable notice inviting objections from the general public.
   - **Objection Hearing & Summary Trial**: If contested, court conducts summary inquiry into right to certificate.
   - **Ad-Valorem Court Fee & Stamp Duty**: Judicial stamp duty levied per state Court Fees Act (typically 2% to 6% of the asset schedule valuation).
   - **Indemnity Bond & Surety**: Court may order petitioner to execute an indemnity bond with sureties before certificate delivery.
