# Domicile, Residence, and Nativity Certificate Guide — Research Notes

**Prepared:** 5 November 2026

**Scope:** Constitutional citizenship principles (single citizenship under Indian Constitution), State revenue rules governing Domicile, Permanent Residence (PRC), and Nativity Certificates, minimum continuous residency requirements, and education/recruitment applicability.

## 1. Domicile vs. Nativity vs. Residence Certificates

These three certificates are **not interchangeable**; each serves a distinct legal purpose:
- **Domicile Certificate:** Certifies permanent home/intention to reside permanently in a particular State/UT. Required for state quota in professional colleges (e.g. 85% state quota in NEET UG/PG) and state government recruitment.
- **Nativity Certificate:** Certifies place of birth or ancestral origin in a particular State/UT, irrespective of where the person currently lives.
- **Ordinary Residence Certificate:** Certifies that the applicant currently resides at a specific local address (often required for local ration cards, voter registration, or utility connections). It does not confer permanent domicile rights.

---

## 2. Minimum Residence Requirements by State/UT

There is no universal pan-India residence duration; each State/UT specifies its statutory threshold:
- **Maharashtra:** Minimum 15 years of continuous residence in the state.
- **Tamil Nadu (Nativity):** Place of birth or continuous residence for minimum 5 years; nativity certificate has lifetime validity.
- **Uttar Pradesh / Bihar:** Continuous residence for minimum 3 years or ancestral domicile.
- **Jammu & Kashmir / Ladakh:** 15 years of residence, or 7 years of study with Class 10/12 exams in the UT.
- **Karnataka:** Minimum 7 to 10 consecutive academic years of schooling in Karnataka for state quota.

---

## 3. Evidence Checklist

1. School records / Bonafide certificates showing continuous education across the statutory period.
2. Ancestral land ownership documents (Registered Sale Deed, Khatiyan, Jamabandi, Property Tax receipts).
3. Electricity bills, water connection receipts, or telephone bills covering the required duration.
4. Birth Certificate of applicant showing place of birth in the state.
5. Parents' domicile certificate or service book records (if parents are state/central government employees).
6. Sworn domicile affidavit affirming permanent residence and declaring no duplicate domicile in another State.

---

## 4. Single Domicile Principle

Under Supreme Court rulings (such as *Pradeep Jain v. Union of India*), an Indian citizen can possess only **one domicile of choice** at any given point in time. Holding domicile certificates from two different states simultaneously is legally impermissible and causes disqualification during institutional admissions.
